Why Does Gameplay Lag While Recording?

To fix the problem, we first need to understand it. When you play a heavy game like Valorant, GTA V, or CS:GO, your CPU (processor) and GPU (graphics card) are already working at maximum capacity to render the 3D environment and maintain a playable frame rate.

When you open a standard screen recorder in the background, it starts grabbing frames from your screen and actively compressing them into an MP4 video format in real-time. Video compression (also known as encoding) is an incredibly heavy mathematical process. Because your CPU is now forced to run the game AND compress a video simultaneously, it chokes. This bottleneck causes your game's FPS to drop from 60 to 15, resulting in a laggy recording and an unplayable gaming experience.

The Solution: Stop Real-Time Compression

The secret to zero-lag recording on a low-end PC is to separate the capturing process from the encoding process. Instead of forcing your PC to make an MP4 video while you are gaming, you need software that simply saves raw screen data to your hard drive, using almost zero CPU power.

This is exactly where Any Screen Recorder comes into play. Developed specifically for low-end hardware, it completely bypasses the traditional recording method.

Best Settings for Recording on a Potato PC

Even with the best software, configuring your Windows settings correctly is vital for maximum performance. Follow these steps before you start recording:

1. Lower Your In-Game Resolution

If you are playing on a very old laptop, do not force your game to run at 1080p. Lower the in-game resolution to 720p (1280x720). Any Screen Recorder will capture the native resolution, giving you a smooth 720p 60FPS video which is perfectly acceptable for YouTube.

2. Disable Windows Background Apps

Before launching your game, open your Windows Task Manager and close heavy background applications like Google Chrome, Discord, or Spotify. Every megabyte of RAM counts when you only have 2GB or 4GB available.

3. Turn Off Windows Game Bar

Windows 10 and 11 come with a built-in "Xbox Game Bar" that constantly records in the background (Background Recording feature). This actively steals your FPS. Go to Windows Settings > Gaming > Captures, and turn off "Record in the background while I'm playing a game."
